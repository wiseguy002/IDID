About iDID

Short (25 words): iDID is a free, private journal for people living with dissociation, DID and trauma responses, and the families and friends who support them.

Long (80 words): iDID helps people living with dissociation, dissociative identity disorder (DID) and trauma responses notice what happens, find what helps, and stay connected to the people they trust. In a few taps, people can record when and where a hard moment happened, possible triggers, mood, and which parts were present. A trust circle can add what they noticed, and the person chooses what each member sees. They can also give their therapist a private, read-only view of exactly what they pick, and remove it any time. Everything is encrypted on the device and shared end-to-end encrypted. iDID is free, ad-free and designed with sensory needs in mind.

Name & pronunciation

Written iDID: a lowercase "i" followed by capital "DID". Said "I did", as in I did it. I noticed. I made it through. Please keep the lowercase "i", including at the start of a sentence.

Reporting on mental health

Thank you for covering dissociation and trauma with care. Please consider:

  • Including crisis resources (call or text 988 in the US) alongside stories about suicide or self-harm.
  • Avoiding the terms "split personality" or "multiple personalities" as shorthand for DID, and avoiding links between DID and violence.
  • Following the Recommendations for Reporting on Suicide.
