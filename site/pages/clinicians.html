For clinicians

Between sessions, a gentle record your client controls

iDID helps people with dissociation, DID/OSDD and trauma responses notice what happens and what helps, and bring that into therapy, on their terms.

What your client can bring you

  • Moments, not just memories. Time and place, possible triggers, mood, feelings, lost time, and which parts were around, logged in a few taps with every field optional.
  • Patterns over weeks. Common triggers, places, times of day, and triggers by part, plus "Who's here" fronting history showing usual times and common hand-overs.
  • Observations from a trusted person. A partner or family member can add what they noticed, which helps when amnesia leaves gaps.
  • Therapy prep. An agenda, questions and wins gathered since the last session, with notes afterward.
  • Coping and safety tools. A safety plan, grounding and breathing, a comfort kit, an inner board for notes between parts, and "What did I miss?" for after lost time.

How sharing with you works

Your client chooses exactly what you see (for example mood trends and triggers, but not free-text notes), reads a plain-language consent summary, and sends you a link. You open a read-only view in any browser, with no account to create. They can see when it was opened, set an end date, and remove your access at any time. They can also print a clinician summary or export a spreadsheet to bring to a session.

The view is encrypted on your client's device with a key that lives only in the link, so iDID's servers can't read it. Please keep the link private, as you would any clinical note.

Who it suits

  • Adults and teens (13+) with dissociative symptoms, DID/OSDD, PTSD or C-PTSD who find self-monitoring helpful.
  • Systems who want parts to have a voice: parts can be named, sign entries and inner-board notes, and have their own plant in the Mood Garden. iDID lets each person choose their own words: parts, alters, headmates or selves.
  • Families and partners who want to support without taking over. Access is set per person, and the circle can be handed to the person it's about.

Tracking isn't right for everyone or at every stage. For some clients, logging can increase focus on symptoms. Everything in iDID is optional and nothing is scored or streaked, but please use your clinical judgment about whether and when to suggest it.

What iDID isn't

  • Not a medical device or treatment. It doesn't diagnose, assess risk, or replace care.
  • Not monitored. No one at iDID reads entries or responds to crises. The app shows 988 and 911 prominently, and clients can keep a safety plan.
  • Not part of your record system. iDID isn't a covered entity or business associate under HIPAA, and we don't make HIPAA claims. What your client shares with you is theirs to share. Once you've viewed it, handle it as your own practice requires.

Privacy, briefly

Journals are encrypted on the device with the client's passphrase. If they use the optional online circle, records are end-to-end encrypted, and some kinds (the inner board, fronting log, voice notes, body and medication notes) are always private to the client. There are no ads, trackers or data sales. See the privacy policy for exactly what our servers can see.

Suggesting iDID to a client

  1. Suggest they open app.idid.care on their phone and add it to their home screen. It's free, with no app store needed.
  2. They can explore the sample circle first, without entering anything real.
  3. If it fits, agree together on what would be useful to track, and whether to share a view with you.

Printable materials are in the press kit, and plain-language background on dissociation is on the Learn page.
