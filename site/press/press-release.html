For immediate release

IDID launches a free, end-to-end encrypted journal for people living with dissociation and trauma responses, and the circles that support them

New app helps people with DID and related experiences notice patterns, find what helps, and stay connected to trusted family, friends and clinicians, with privacy built in from the start.

[CITY, STATE], November 12, 2026: IDID today announced the launch of IDID (said "I did"), a free web app at idid.care that gives people living with dissociation, dissociative identity disorder (DID) and trauma responses a calm, private way to record what happens and to share it with the people who help them.

Dissociation can cause lost time and memory gaps, which make it hard to explain experiences to a therapist or loved one. IDID lets people note a moment in a few taps: when and where it happened, possible triggers, mood, how long it lasted, which parts were present, and who might have noticed. Trusted circle members can be gently asked to add what they observed, helping fill in the gaps. Over time, IDID shows patterns, such as common triggers, places and times, and which coping ideas actually help, and produces a summary people can bring to appointments.

Privacy is central to IDID's design. Journals are encrypted on the person's own device with a passphrase only they know. When shared with a circle, records are encrypted end-to-end, so IDID's servers store only data they cannot read. IDID carries no advertising, uses no trackers and never sells data.

Key features

  • Quick, optional logging of moments, triggers, mood, fronting and co-fronting parts, lost time and duration.
  • A trust circle of family, friends or clinicians who can add their own observations.
  • Patterns and clinician summaries showing what tends to come before hard moments and what helps.
  • Pause tools and gentle check-ins: breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding, orientation, and kind prompts to breathe, try a personal ritual, or get some fresh air.
  • Find support: crisis lines, dissociation and trauma specialists, low-cost care and community resources matched to a person's ZIP code and needs.
  • Appointments and reminders for therapy, counselling and support groups, with calendar export.
  • Kindness: anonymous, curated notes of encouragement between people using IDID, and a daily small act of kindness to pay it forward.
  • Sensory-friendly design: calm colours including dark and high-contrast themes, adjustable text, a calm-motion mode, and no sounds or flashing.

Availability

IDID is available now, free, at app.idid.care on any modern phone, tablet or computer, and can be added to a phone's home screen. Native Android and iOS apps are planned.

IDID is a journaling and support tool and does not diagnose or treat any condition. People in crisis in the US can call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, at any time.

About IDID
