For immediate release

iDID launches a free, end-to-end encrypted journal for people living with dissociation and trauma responses, and the circles that support them

New app helps people with DID and related experiences notice patterns, find what helps, and stay connected to trusted family, friends and clinicians, with privacy built in from the start.

BATON ROUGE, La., November 12, 2026: iDID today announced the launch of iDID (said "I did"), a free web app at idid.care that gives people living with dissociation, dissociative identity disorder (DID) and trauma responses a calm, private way to record what happens and to share it with the people who help them.

Dissociation can cause lost time and memory gaps, which make it hard to explain experiences to a therapist or loved one. iDID lets people note a moment in a few taps: when and where it happened, possible triggers, mood, how long it lasted, which parts were present, and who might have noticed. Trusted circle members can be gently asked to add what they observed, helping fill in the gaps. Over time, iDID shows patterns, such as common triggers, places and times, and which coping ideas actually help. People can give their therapist a private, read-only view of exactly what they choose, which stays current between sessions and can be removed at any time.

"Care works best when everyone sees the same picture. iDID gives people with DID and trauma responses a calm, private place to record what happens, and a safe way to bring it to their therapist and their circle," said the iDID team.

Privacy is central to iDID's design. Journals are encrypted on the person's own device with a passphrase only they know. When shared with a circle, records are encrypted end-to-end, so iDID's servers store only data they cannot read. iDID carries no advertising, uses no trackers and never sells data. When a circle's owner removes someone, the circle's encryption key is changed, so their old copy can't open anything written afterward.

Key features

  • Quick, optional logging of moments, triggers, mood, fronting and co-fronting parts, lost time and duration, with a timeline and "looking back" reflections.
  • A trust circle the person controls: invite family and friends with a one-time private link, see who has joined, and send a reminder if an invite was missed. The circle's owner chooses what each person can see and do (view and add, view only, or only selected information) and can hand the circle to the person it's about. Members can add what they noticed.
  • Sharing with a therapist: a private, read-only view of exactly what the person chooses, such as patterns, moments, a safety plan or therapy prep notes. Nothing is shared without explicit agreement to a plain summary, the view stays current, the person can see when it was last opened, and access can be removed at any time. No account is needed to view it.
  • Patterns and clinician summaries showing what tends to come before hard moments and what helps, plus printable summaries and spreadsheets.
  • Pause tools and gentle check-ins: breathing, box breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding, orientation, a wordless Calm space, and kind prompts to breathe, try a personal ritual, or get some fresh air.
  • Mood Garden: a plant for each part of a system, or for each person in the circle, that grows from check-ins, pauses, kind notes and moments noticed. Weather follows recent check-ins, so hard days bring wind and rain, but plants bend and keep growing and nothing ever wilts. Gentle ideas suggest the next small step, and the garden acknowledges the work already done.
  • Who's here: a two-tap fronting log for people with DID and OSDD. Notes that one part leaves for another on the inner board appear when that part arrives, and gentle patterns show who tends to be around when.
  • What did I miss?: after lost time, iDID grounds people in the present and gathers what was noted during the gap.
  • Voice notes for when typing is too much, encrypted like everything else, and an optional weekly reflection that is never scored.
  • Supporting someone: a guide for partners, family and friends with words to try when someone dissociates.
  • Tools for hard days: a guided safety plan, "Check on me" to quietly ask trusted people to reach out, a comfort kit, a private inner board where parts can leave notes for each other, body basics with medication reminders, and one-tap quick hide.
  • Find support: crisis lines, dissociation and trauma specialists, low-cost care and community resources matched to a person's ZIP code and needs, plus saved support contacts.
  • Appointments and therapy prep for therapy, counseling and support groups, with reminders, calendar export, and a summary of what has happened since the last session.
  • Kindness: anonymous, curated notes of encouragement between people using iDID, and a daily small act of kindness to pay it forward.
  • Easy to start and to keep going: a quick tour, Help & guides, Explore with gentle badges, and the option to pick up on a new device with an email code and passphrase.
  • English and Español (beta): setup, sign-in, Pause, the safety plan and crisis information are available in Spanish, including the 988 Lifeline's Spanish options.
  • Sensory-friendly design: calm colors including dark and high-contrast themes, adjustable text, a calm-motion mode, and no sounds or flashing.

Availability

iDID is available now, free, at app.idid.care on any modern phone, tablet or computer, in English and Spanish (beta), and can be added to a phone's home screen. People who want to help keep it running can contribute through Ko-fi; contributions never unlock features. Clinicians can learn more at idid.care/clinicians. Native Android and iOS apps are planned.

iDID is a journaling and support tool and does not diagnose or treat any condition. People in crisis in the US can call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, at any time.

About iDID

iDID is a free, private journal for people living with dissociation, DID and trauma responses, and for the families and friends who support them. Learn more at idid.care.
